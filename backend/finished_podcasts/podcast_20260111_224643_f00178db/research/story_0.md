## Core_summary
OpenAI has launched **ChatGPT Health**, a consumer-facing “medical assistant” inside ChatGPT that lets users **securely connect electronic medical records and data from popular wellness and wearable platforms** (e.g., Apple Health, MyFitnessPal, Weight Watchers, Peloton, Function Health) via infrastructure provider **b.well**, so the system can contextualize health and wellness answers to an individual’s real data while explicitly **not providing diagnoses or treatment decisions** and positioning itself as an adjunct to, not a replacement for, clinicians.[1][2][4][6] OpenAI and its executives frame the product as a **private space for health conversations** where the model can summarize visit notes, explain lab trends, help prepare doctor questions, and tailor lifestyle guidance, built in “close collaboration” with hundreds of physicians and informed by prior evaluations (over **600,000 model outputs across 30 clinical areas** reviewed by a 260‑physician network, plus a dedicated **Health Bench** for realistic scenario testing), and launched initially to a small early-access cohort before broader rollout to a user base that already generates **tens to hundreds of millions of health-related queries per week**.[1][3][5][7]

Expert commentary and early analysis highlight both **potential benefits and serious risks**: research cited by proponents notes that ChatGPT has **matched or exceeded physicians in diagnostic accuracy in controlled studies**, suggesting that adding longitudinal records and wearable data could further improve reasoning quality, while health-system partners report gains in administrative efficiency and patient comprehension when using related OpenAI tools to summarize records and translate medical jargon.[2][3][7] At the same time, ethicists and industry observers emphasize unresolved concerns around **data privacy, consent, security, liability, and bias**, warning that centralizing sensitive health data in a commercial AI platform could create new attack surfaces and trust issues, that model errors or overreliance by patients might lead to delayed or inappropriate care despite disclaimers, and that regulatory frameworks (such as those governing medical devices and decision-support tools) may need to evolve if systems like ChatGPT Health become a de facto **“agentic” health interface** that intermediates how people understand and act on medical information at scale; many foresee this move accelerating AI integration into healthcare workflows, forcing providers, payers, and consumer health brands to adapt as the locus of health decision support shifts toward continuously updated, data-rich AI assistants integrated into both clinical enterprises (**ChatGPT for Healthcare** in hospitals) and everyday consumer health management.[1][2][3][4][5][6]
