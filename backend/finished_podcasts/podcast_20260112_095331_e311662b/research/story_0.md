## Core_summary
AI data centers require a $3-7 trillion global buildout by 2030 to address power and compute bottlenecks driven by surging AI workloads, with hyperscalers projected to spend $527 billion in 2026 capex alone, potentially rising to $700 billion to match historical tech cycles.[1][2][3][4][5] Experts like Steve Carlini (Schneider Electric) describe facilities evolving into "AI factories" for continuous training/inference, demanding tripled rack densities (45-100 kW), 80% liquid cooling adoption, and $30M/MW costs, amid 100 GW new capacity adding $1.2T in real estate value, mostly in Americas.[1][2] Power constraints outpace aging U.S. grids, spurring on-site generation, battery storage (recoverable in 3-5 years via incentives), grid stabilization via load flexibility, and regional/hybrid deployments over centralized clouds; AI could handle 50% of workloads by 2030, doubling global capacity to 156 GW while pressuring networks for bandwidth/latency upgrades.[1][2][3] Goldman Sachs notes consensus capex estimates consistently underestimate (50%+ actual growth vs. 20% projected in 2024-25), with consolidation weeding out smaller developers; future implications include autonomous operations, digital twins for design, edge computing, and AI-driven grid modernization, though enterprises face readiness gaps in sovereign AI scaling.[1][2][3][4]
