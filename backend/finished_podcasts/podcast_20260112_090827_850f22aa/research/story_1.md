## Core_summary
The University of Michigan's preliminary January 2026 consumer sentiment index rose to **54.0**, up from 52.9 in December 2025 and exceeding expectations of 53.5, marking the highest since September 2025 and a second straight monthly gain, though **25% below January 2025 levels** and near the all-time low of 50 from June 2022.[1][2] Gains concentrated among lower-income households, while higher-income sentiment declined; inflation worries dominate despite CPI at 2.7% (prices up 25% over five years), with year-ahead expectations steady at **4.2%** (lowest in nearly a year, vs. 3.3% last January) and long-term at 3.4% (up from 3.2%).[1][2]

**Expert views**: Survey director Joanne Hsu notes historical lows driven by inflation's cumulative stress and job concerns, despite recent upticks; tariff fears from Trump plans post-December 2024 peak (74) are easing.[1] Conference Board data shows related December pessimism in business conditions (18.7% "good" vs. 21% prior), family finances turning negative, but positive shifts in future outlooks, stock expectations, and recession odds (1/5 say "not likely").[3]

**Implications**: Trading Economics forecasts 54 end-Q1 2026, trending to 58 by 2027 amid softening labor market; Fed's third 2025 rate cut aided mild optimism, though rate rise expectations grew.[2][3]
