## Core_summary
CES 2026 highlighted a broad shift toward **AI-centric, energy-efficient, and spatial computing technologies**, with major chipmakers (Nvidia Rubin platform, Intel Core Ultra Series 3 “Panther Lake,” AMD Ryzen AI 400/Ryzen AI Max+, Qualcomm Snapdragon X2 Plus) focusing on lowering AI inference and training costs (e.g., Rubin claims 10x cheaper inference tokens and 4x fewer GPUs for MoE training than Blackwell) and bringing larger on‑device models to laptops and edge devices for privacy, latency, and cost benefits.[2][3][4] Industry experts and exhibitors emphasized AI as infrastructure for “AI factories” and “industrial AI,” with Siemens, Nvidia, Lenovo and others showcasing digital twins, industrial metaverse tools, autonomous robots, computer-vision safety systems, and spatial-hearing/cognition AI for vehicles, while consumer-facing reveals centered on AI PCs, foldable phones, AR glasses, health and wellness wearables, and emotion-tracking AI jewelry—all raising future implications around energy demand, workforce augmentation, data privacy, and the normalization of ubiquitous, context-aware AI in both enterprise and daily life.[2][3][4][5][1]
