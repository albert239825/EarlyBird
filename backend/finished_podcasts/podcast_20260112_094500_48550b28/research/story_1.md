## Core_summary
China's EAST tokamak reactor, dubbed the "artificial sun," exceeded the **Greenwald Limit**—a longstanding plasma density barrier—achieving stable densities of 1.3–1.65 times the limit (vs. usual 0.8–1 range) via **plasma-wall self-organization**, controlled initial fuel gas pressure, and **electron cyclotron resonance heating (ECRH)** during ohmic start-up, as detailed in a Jan 2026 *Science Advances* study by the Chinese Academy of Sciences (CAS).[1][2][5]

**Expert opinions**: Co-lead author Prof. **Ping Zhu** (Huazhong Univ. of Science & Tech.) states findings offer a "practical and scalable pathway" for density limits in tokamaks and burning plasma devices like ITER, enabling higher energy outputs toward ignition; team plans EAST validation under high-performance conditions.[1][2]

**Related events**: Builds on EAST's 2025 >1,000-second plasma record (later surpassed by France's WEST); parallels tungsten advances in WEST (6 min at 50M°C, Apr 2024) and KSTAR (100M°C sustainment).[1][2][4]

**Stats/data**: Plasma stable beyond empirical ceiling, previously causing instability; no ignition yet (self-sustaining fusion), but advances confinement for prolonged burns.[1][2][5]

**Future implications**: Accelerates path to scalable fusion power plants (e.g., ITER full reactions by 2039); complements private efforts like Helion's 50MW deal with Microsoft by 2028, though commercial viability needs further materials/ignition breakthroughs.[1][2]
