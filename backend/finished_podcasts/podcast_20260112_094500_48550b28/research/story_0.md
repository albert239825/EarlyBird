## Core_summary
**CES 2026: Physical AI and Robotics Dominate**

CES 2026 marks a fundamental industry shift from consumer gadgets toward **physical AI systems and autonomous robotics** as practical deployment technologies[1][2][3][4].

**Key Developments:**

**Autonomous Vehicles & AI Infrastructure**
Tesla's next-generation AI5 chip delivers 40x faster AI performance than prior generations[1]. NVIDIA's DRIVE Thor platform powers Level 4 robotaxis, including WeRide's GXR and platforms from autonomy companies Nuro, Wayve, and Zoox[1]. Physical AI models trained in hyper-realistic virtual environments enable autonomous systems to handle rare scenarios like extreme weather[2]. 5G-enabled C-V2X communication facilitates real-time vehicle-to-vehicle and vehicle-to-infrastructure coordination[2].

**Humanoid Robotics at Scale**
Humanoid robots have moved beyond conceptual stages to commercial deployment[1]. Companies including Agility Robotics, AGIBOT, and Galbot have produced thousands of units deployed in manufacturing, retail, and warehouse operations[1]. Boston Dynamics' Atlas prototype features 56 degrees of freedom with fluid locomotion; Hyundai plans to deploy Atlas in car plants by 2028 with a target of 30,000 robots annually[4]. NEURA and Agibot showcased refined humanoid ecosystems designed for real-world work[3].

**Integrated Platforms**
NVIDIA's Jetson Thor running on Arm-based compute enables simulation-to-deployment workflows[1]. Arm-based infrastructure through AWS Graviton and Google Cloud partnerships accelerates cloud-to-production integration[1]. Robots now function across applications including hospital care, assisted living, and autonomous airport operations[3].

**Market Shift**
The convergence represents a transition from personal device ownership toward cloud-connected, subscription-based autonomous services and on-demand AI-enhanced transportation[2]. This reflects broader industry recognition that AI's value now derives from infrastructure and physical system deployment rather than consumer hardware novelty[1][3][4].
