## Core_summary
CES 2026 highlights tech's pivot from consumer gadgets to **AI infrastructure** and **robotics**, with **physical AI** enabling real-world deployment in vehicles, humanoids, and homes.[1][2][3][4] Key showcases include Tesla's Arm-based AI5 chip (40x faster than prior gen), NVIDIA DRIVE Thor in WeRide's Level 4 Robotaxi, and Jetson Thor for scalable robotics; humanoids like Boston Dynamics' Atlas (56 degrees of freedom, planned for Hyundai plants in 2028 with 30,000-unit factory goal), NEURA's multi-platform ecosystem, AgiBot's modular lineup (thousands deployed in manufacturing/retail), and companion bots like Tombot.[1][2][3] Expert views from CTA's Senior Director of Innovation emphasize **physical AI** as smarter, functional robots evolving from vacuums to humanoids, alongside CES Foundry sessions on AI/quantum infrastructure; Arm notes efficiency/scalability via AWS/Google Cloud integrations.[1][4][5] Stats: Agility Robotics/AGIBOT/Galbot with thousands of units in commercial use; policy talks address AI regulation challenges for autonomy/wearables.[1][3][5] Implications: Shift to edge AI accelerates Level 4 autonomy (e.g., Nuro/Wayve/Zoox zones, pedestrian detection via layered sensors), robotics in warehouses/hospitals/airports/plants, and digital twins for faster production—marking transition from demos to scalable deployment.[1][2][3]
